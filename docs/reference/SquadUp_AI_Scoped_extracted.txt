Project Submission | Author: Nithish Kumar S | Year: 2026
1. Problem Statement
Almost every village and town in Tamil Nadu fields its own volleyball and cricket teams, often several per town
under different organisations. These teams are shrinking. Senior players retire with no replacements, and
players move to other cities for work and can no longer turn up regularly.
This creates two problems that today are solved only by phone calls and WhatsApp forwards:
  Teams short by 2-3 players cannot fill a match, and often cannot register for a tournament at all.
 Players whose whole team has dissolved have nowhere to go and no reliable way to find a guest slot.
The talent and the vacancies both exist, but they never meet. WhatsApp posts vanish within a day, nothing can
be filtered by role or experience, nobody can tell who is reliable, and information only reaches people already in
the right group. The result is forfeited entries, players quitting the sport, and rural talent that stays invisible.

2. Our Solution
SquadUp is a mobile-first web app (Tamil and English) that works as a two-way marketplace with a trust layer.
Teams register and post vacancies, players build profiles and mark themselves available, and both sides find
each other in a live directory. Consistency is proven through GPS check-ins confirmed by the captain, and a
calendar heat map. Tournaments are published on a shared board so every team hears about them.
Design rule: the normal app (the core) decides what is allowed and what is true. The AI layer only
understands, ranks, explains and drafts. If the AI is unavailable, the app still works.
One account for everyone. Every user is a player and every feature is open to every player. Anyone can
create a team, post a vacancy, invite players or publish a tournament. Team actions such as editing a roster or
confirming attendance are limited to the owner and managers of that team. Admin approval only adds a Verified
Organiser badge to tournaments.
Core features (no AI)
  Phone OTP login, team and roster management, vacancy board with status tracking
 Player profile with availability, travel radius and privacy controls (phone hidden until both sides accept)
 Looking for a team option: a player marks themselves as permanent, guest or tournament-only and appears
in the Players Available directory
 Two-way matching by filters (role, area, date, slot) with notifications to both sides
 Invites, requests, accept / decline / counter, guest slot booking, in-app chat
 Tournament board (info only), ground directory, district leaderboards, admin moderation

3. Deep Learning Component
Deep Learning is kept minimal and uses a transformer model only. It does one job that a normal rule or an API
call cannot do well.
3.1. Tanglish / Tamil Vacancy Parser (flagship)
Type
Deep Learning - fine-tuned transformer (intent detection + slot extraction)
What it solves
Village captains do not want to fill an 8-field form. They type the way they speak, mixing Tamil
and English in English letters (Tanglish), with spelling that changes from person to person.
Normal keyword rules break on this. The parser turns one messy line into a clean structured
vacancy, so the captain only taps confirm.
Example
Captain types: "sunday morning peelamedu 2 spikers 1 setter venum"
Parser output:
  Intent = post_vacancy
  Vacancy 1: role = spiker, count = 2
  Vacancy 2: role = setter, count = 1
  Date = next Sunday, time = morning, place = Peelamedu, urgency = normal
The vacancy form is filled automatically. The same model also spots "setter, Saravanampatti,
weekends, looking for a team" as a looking_for_team post and fills a player availability profile.
How it works
 Data: generate 2-3k synthetic Tanglish posts with Gemini, hand-correct about 300, and use
these as the gold set
 Model: fine-tune a multilingual Indian-language transformer (MuRIL or IndicBERT) with two
heads: an intent classifier (post_vacancy, looking_for_team, other) and a token tagger for
slots (role, count, date, time, place, urgency)
 Post-processing: normalise roles to fixed labels, resolve relative dates such as "sunday",
and match place names to the ground directory despite spelling variations
 Evaluation: intent accuracy and per-slot F1 on a held-out set
 Serving: FastAPI endpoint POST /parse/post, with confidence score. If confidence is low, the
form opens pre-filled for manual fixing

4. Generative AI Components
All Generative AI features are powered by the Gemini API. Every call uses a Pydantic schema for structured
output, and Gemini never invents data: it only works on records returned by the core.
4.1. Vic - Conversational Orchestration Agent (Text + Voice)
Type
Generative AI - LangGraph agent with Gemini function calling; text is primary, voice is an
optional mic button
What it solves
The app has many features (parser, matcher, invites, translation, rules, tournaments). Finding
the right screen and filling forms is a barrier for village users. Vic is one chat that can reach
every other feature. The user says what they want in one sentence, by typing or by tapping the
mic, and Vic runs the right steps and asks for approval before anything is sent.
Example
Captain (typed or spoken): "We are short a wicketkeeper for the 15th"
1. Vic calls parse_vacancy (transformer) and gets role = wicketkeeper, count = 1, date = 15th
2. Calls search_players and finds 14 eligible players
3. Calls rank_candidates (AI Matcher) and gets the top 3 with reasons
4. Shows: "Here are 3 players. Send invites?"
5. Captain taps yes and send_invite runs.
Player: "Find me a volleyball team near Saravanampatti for Sunday" - Vic searches vacancies,
ranks them and drafts a request for approval.
Other requests Vic routes: "Is this ball out?" goes to the Rules and Umpire Assistant, "Translate
this post" goes to Translation.
How it works
 Routing: Gemini reads each message, picks the intent and LangGraph runs the steps as a
graph of tool calls
 Tools (each is a FastAPI function) and the feature behind each: parse_vacancy (Vacancy
Parser), search_players / search_teams (core filters), rank_candidates (4.2 AI Matcher),
draft_invite (4.3 Invites), translate_post (4.4 Translation), ask_rules / give_ruling (4.5 Rules
and Umpire), recommend_tournaments (4.6 Tournament Recommender), plus send_invite,
get_profile and update_profile
 Text (primary): a normal chat box. Every feature is usable by text alone
 Voice (optional): a mic button in the chat. The user taps it and speaks in Tamil, English or
Tanglish. Gemini's audio input converts the speech to text, the transcript is shown in the chat
so the user can fix it, and from there it follows the same path as a typed message. A speaker
button on a reply reads it aloud using text-to-speech
 Approval rule: anything that sends a message or changes data needs a confirmation tap,
and every tool checks team permission. Vic can only use the tools listed, so it cannot invent
actions
 Vic is only a chat wrapper: it calls the same core functions the app buttons call
Tech stack
LangGraph, Gemini API (function calling, audio input for speech-to-text, text-to-speech for
replies), FastAPI, Pydantic, PostgreSQL, Redis (conversation state), browser audio recording
(MediaRecorder) in the PWA
4.2. AI Matcher (rerank + "why this player")
Type
Generative AI - LLM reranking with structured output, works in both directions

What it solves
Filters can say who is eligible but not who is the best fit. The captain also will not trust a stranger
without reasons. The matcher ranks the shortlist by weighing role fit, playing style, distance,
availability and reliability together, and explains each ranking in one line. It handles fuzzy
wording that filters miss, for example "strong attacker" versus "outside hitter".
Example
Vacancy: 2 spikers, Peelamedu, Sunday morning, friendly match. Core filters return 14 players.
Gemini returns:
1. Arun (score 92) - outside hitter, 3 km away, free Sunday morning, reliability 91%
2. Karthik (score 85) - spiker, 6 km away, played 5 friendly matches
3. Vijay (score 78) - middle blocker who can play spiker, 8 km away
Reverse direction: a player looking for a team sees ranked teams with a reason like "needs a
setter, 4 km away, plays on your free days".
How it works
 Step 1 (core): SQL and PostGIS filters produce an eligible shortlist of 10-20. Gemini never
invents players
 Step 2: send only the shortlisted candidates (with IDs) and the vacancy to Gemini
 Step 3: Gemini returns a fixed JSON list of player_id, score and reason, validated by
Pydantic
 Step 4: the captain approves and the core notifies the top players
 Reliability figures come from the core formula, so the explanation is always traceable to real
records
Tech stack
Gemini API (structured output), PostgreSQL + PostGIS (shortlist filters), FastAPI, Pydantic
4.3. Auto-drafted Invites and Follow-ups
Type
Generative AI - prompt-based generation
What it solves
Captains lose time writing polite messages, and players often do not reply to short or unclear
messages. The feature writes the message for the captain, in the right language, with all details,
and the captain only reviews and sends. It also writes the player's request message when a
player approaches a team.
Example
Captain selects Arun. Draft: "Hi Arun, our team Peelamedu Warriors needs a spiker this
Sunday, 7 AM, at Peelamedu ground (friendly match). Are you free?"
If Arun has not replied after a day, a follow-up draft is suggested: "Just checking if you can join
on Sunday. We can also adjust the timing."
How it works
 Core passes vacancy details, player name, language and tone to Gemini
 Gemini returns a short draft in Tamil, English or Tanglish, based on the recipient's language
 Captain edits or approves, then the core sends it via push, SMS or WhatsApp link
 Follow-ups are triggered by a scheduled job when an invite has no reply
Tech stack
Gemini API, FastAPI, Celery (scheduled follow-ups), Firebase push, SMS, WhatsApp Business
API

4.5. Rules and Umpire Assistant (RAG)
Type
Generative AI - Retrieval-Augmented Generation + scenario reasoning
What it solves
Rules and tournament conditions are scattered across PDFs, posters and WhatsApp messages,
and they differ from tournament to tournament. Teams miss conditions and argue during
matches. This feature works in two ways: it answers rule questions from the real documents,
and in Umpire mode the user explains what happened and the assistant understands the
scenario and gives a decision with the reason.
Example
Rule question: "Is a libero allowed in this tournament?" Answer: "Yes. Rule 4.2 of the
tournament conditions allows one libero per team." (source shown).
Umpire mode (volleyball): "Our libero set the ball with his fingers from in front of the 3-metre
line and our spiker hit it above the net. Is that allowed?" Decision: "Not allowed - fault, point to
the opponent." Reason: a ball set overhand by the libero in front of the attack line cannot be
attacked above net height. Confidence: high.
Umpire mode (cricket): "Ball pitched in line, hit the batter's pad in line with the stumps, no shot
played, and it would have hit the stumps." Decision: "Out - LBW." If a fact is missing (for
example "where did the ball pitch?"), it asks one question before deciding.
How it works
 Knowledge base: official volleyball and cricket rulebooks plus each tournament's posted
conditions (added by the organiser as text or PDF). Documents are split into chunks and
indexed
 Step 1, understand: Gemini turns the user's story into a structured scenario: sport, event,
players involved, facts found and facts missing
 Step 2, ask if unclear: if a key fact is missing, one short question is asked
 Step 3, retrieve: the most relevant rule chunks are fetched. Tournament conditions override
the general rulebook, and the answer says when they do
 Step 4, decide: Gemini reasons only from the retrieved rule text and returns a fixed JSON:
decision, rule reference, plain explanation, confidence and an escalate flag
 Safety: rulings are advice for friendly and local matches, and the on-field umpire's decision is
final. With low confidence or no rule found, it says so and refers the case to the umpire or
organiser instead of guessing
 Works in Tamil and English, and is also reachable through Vic by text or voice
Tech stack
Gemini API (generation, embeddings, structured output), pgvector, FastAPI, Pydantic (scenario
and ruling schemas), LangChain / LangGraph retriever, PDF text extraction

Type

What it solves
Teams only hear about tournaments if they are in the right WhatsApp group, and many events
have registration deadlines they miss. The recommender looks at a team's level, location and
calendar and suggests the events that really fit.
Example
Team: Peelamedu Warriors, volleyball, district level, free weekends in November.
Recommendation: "District Cup, 15-16 Nov, 12 km away, open to district-level teams,
registration closes in 4 days. Match: high." An event for state-level teams is not shown, because
it does not fit.
How it works
 Core filters tournaments by sport, district and date range
 Gemini compares each remaining event with the team profile (level, past matches, roster size,
free dates) and returns a fit reason and deadline flag
 Result is shown on the tournament board and also through Vic ("Any tournaments this month
for our team?")
 If a team is short of players for a tournament, the agent links it to the AI Matcher (4.2) to find
the missing players
Tech stack
Gemini API (structured output), PostgreSQL, FastAPI, Celery (deadline alerts)
5. Summary
SquadUp combines 1 Deep Learning transformer (the Tanglish Vacancy Parser) with 6 Generative AI features
(Vic the Orchestration Agent with text and voice, AI Matcher, Auto-drafted Invites, Translation, Rules and
Umpire Assistant, Tournament Recommender), on top of a normal, dependable core, with 0 classic ML models.
The design is trust-first: the core holds the truth, the transformer understands Tanglish text, and Gemini ranks,
explains and drafts. Everything is open to every player, with one account type. The pilot starts with 15-20
personally onboarded Coimbatore teams, so the directory is never empty, before expanding to more sports and
all of Tamil Nadu.
SquadUp connects village and town sports teams with the players they are missing, using verified consistency
and Tamil-first AI, so no team forfeits and no player is left without a game.
Scope note
Deferred to a later version: photo-based check-in verification (Vision Transformer), Team Composition
